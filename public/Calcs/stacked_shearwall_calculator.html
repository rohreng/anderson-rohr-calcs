How to use: Levels stack top to bottom; the last panel is the base level at the foundation. Enter PW and PE = the strength-level, incremental force delivered to this wall line at each level. The engine applies 0.6W, 0.7E and 0.6D, runs both cases in full, and reports the controlling one per check. Give each wall its full length L, a Method, the list of full-height segment widths bi, and an opening schedule (width × clear height). Perforated (§4.3.2.3): one wall with openings, Co from the opening areas, vmax = V/(Co·Σbi), one hold-down pair at the wall ends and the §4.3.6.4.2.1 uniform uplift at the bottom plate. Segmented (§4.3.2.1): each bi is its own full-height shear wall with its own hold-down pair; story shear is shared in proportion to design capacity with 2b/h on segments of h/b > 2:1 (§4.3.5.5.1 Exc. 1), so veff = V/Σbeff is checked against vASD; openings are gaps between segments (only Σbi + Σw ≤ L is checked), the unsheathed area is ignored, no uniform uplift row, and a segment of h/b > 3.5 is refused. A segmented wall keeps the same segment count on every level it exists. Several walls on one line: the Diaphragm Designer delivers one force per wall line; the split into walls happens here. + line on a row adds a wall to that line (same construction and line force — enter its L and segments); walls with the same Line key share the line's story shear at each level in proportion to design capacity from geometry, Co·Σbi or Σbeff (equal-length opening-free walls: by length), so every wall of the line carries the same unit shear (§4.3.5.5.1 Exc. 1); a wall absent below leaves the whole story shear to the walls present there. Construction (sheathing, end post, hold-down, sill, uplift, method) and the line force are per line — a change on one wall fans out to the others; L, segments, openings, unsheathed area, dead load and label stay per wall. Clear a wall's Line key to make it its own line. ⇩ Copy walls to levels below on a level header copies that level's walls (geometry, method, line, label, construction) onto the same wall ids below, creating them where missing; each lower level keeps its h, level forces, dead loads and transfers, and the base keeps its anchor-bolt sill and HDUE. One-step Undo.

Engineering notes — method, assumptions and limits
  • Inputs are strength level. The engine applies 0.6W (ASCE 7-16 §2.4.1), 0.7E (§2.4.5) and 0.6D on the dead-load resisting moment, and runs wind and seismic in full.
  • Story shear is accumulated as force and converted once at each story with that story's Co·Σbi (perforated) or Σbeff (segmented) (§4.3.6.4.4, §4.3.6.4.1.1). Unit shears are never summed across levels.
  • Method per wall. Perforated (§4.3.2.3, the default) and segmented (§4.3.2.1 individual full-height wall segments; 2015 §4.3.5.1) are selected in the Method column and may be mixed across the walls of a level, but a wall keeps one method on every level it exists.
  • Several walls on one line (§4.3.5.5.1 Exception 1; 2015 §4.3.3.4.1 Exception 1): walls with the same Line key on a level are one wall line. The line force at each level is the one entered on the line's rows (blank = the level force); the line's story shear at a level — the sum of those forces down to it, Vline,k — is split by that level's shares in proportion to design capacity from geometry alone, Co·Σbi for a perforated wall or Σbeff for a segmented one: Vi,k = Vline,k·si,k, so that vmax (or veff) is the same on every wall of the line at every level — the equal-rigidity assumption stated exactly, and the same rule the segmented method applies within a wall. Equal-length opening-free walls therefore split by length. A wall absent below leaves the whole story shear to the walls present there; a wall that ends above the base while its line goes on hands its shear to those walls (a note asks for the transfer to be verified) but its overturning stops with it. Each wall's overturning is Mi,k = Σ over the stories of Vi,m·hm (§4.3.6.4.4), the hold-down detail listing the line shear, share and increment ΔV at each level. The Diaphragm Designer delivers one force per line and is unchanged; the split into walls happens here. Two walls of a line at one level with different entered line forces are refused.
  • Segmented method (§4.3.2.1, §4.3.5.5.1 Exception 1; 2015 §4.3.5.1, §4.3.3.4.1 Exception 1): each bi is an individual full-height shear wall with its own hold-down pair; openings are the gaps between segments (only Σbi + Σ opening widths ≤ L is checked) and the unsheathed-area entry is ignored. Every segment must satisfy Table 4.3.3 (h/b ≤ 3.5 for blocked wood structural panels; 2015 Table 4.3.4) — a narrower one is refused, there is nothing to exclude it into. Story shear is distributed in proportion to the design capacities with the nominal capacity of segments of h/b > 2:1 multiplied by fi = 2bi/h, which "need not be further reduced by the adjustment in 4.3.3.2" — the same factor for wind and seismic (C4.3.3.2). So Vi = V·bifi/Σbeff, vi = veff·fi, and the check vi ≤ fi·vASD on every segment is the single row veff = V/Σbeff ≤ vASD; the sill is checked for max vi. Overturning per segment is Mi = Σ over the stories of Vi,m·hm — the story shear of the segment at the share of that story (§4.3.6.4.4), one load path for shear and moment; Ti = (Mi − 0.6 MR,i)/bi about the compression toe (Eq. 4.3-7 form, lever bi, which is 7–10 % below a rod-to-post-centroid lever), MR,i = w·bi²/2 on every segment and PD,end·bi on segment 1 End 1 only; the hold-down row reports the largest-T segment and passes only when every segment's device qualifies; the end post is checked on the largest Ci. There is no §4.3.6.4.2.1 uniform uplift row and no NDS §12.4 combined row — the segment ends are anchored by their hold-downs (§4.3.6.4.2). The 20 ft height limit and the 2,435 plf cap are perforated limitations and are not applied. A segmented wall keeps the same segment count on every level (segment i above lands on segment i below); differing widths are accepted with a note.
  • Perforated method assumptions (§4.3.2.3): a perforated shear wall segment is present at each end of every wall line (item 2); the nominal unit shear capacity does not exceed 2,435 plf (item 4); collectors run the full length of the wall (item 6); top-of-wall and bottom-of-wall elevations are uniform (item 7); h does not exceed 20 ft (item 8); sheathed areas built with less than the tabulated assembly are counted in Ao (item 9 Exception, entered as "Unsh. sf").
  • Not covered: shear wall and diaphragm deflection and story drift (§4.3.4), the diaphragms themselves, collectors beyond this wall line, concrete anchorage (ACI 318 Chapter 17 — breakout, pryout, edge distance) and anchor-rod steel design. Those are separate calculations.
  • Hardware basis: all values are "160" allowables — the load-duration factor is already included and no further increase is applied. Species-dependent: HDUE hold-downs read the DF/SP or SPF/HF column of Simpson C-C-2026 p. 61 by the framing species (the DF/SP column equals ICC-ES ESR-2330 Table 2B); LTP4 reads the DF/SP (715 lb) or SPF/HF (615 lb) row of C-C-2026 p. 310 by the sill species; nails (8d / 10d / 16d common and box, Table L4) read NDS 2018 Table 12N at ts = 1½" and the lower specific gravity of the sill and framing species (16d common 141 / 154 / 120 lb × 1.6 for DF-L / SP / SPF — the table is for both members of identical G, and the detail names which governed; × p/10D where the penetration is under 10D, fn. 3); SDS ¼×4½ screws and anchor bolts (NDS Table 12E, 1½" sill on concrete with 6" embedment) carry a DF/SP and an SPF value by the sill species. Not species-dependent: coil straps (ESR-2105 Table 4) are DF/SP only and are refused for SPF framing.
  • Bottom-plate uplift (§4.3.6.4.2.1): t = vmax is resisted by the sill fasteners themselves. Nails — NDS Table 12.2C withdrawal at the receiving member's G (framing species) × penetration × CD 1.6, penetration ≥ 6D (§12.1.6.4 nails, §12.1.5.6 wood screws); nail sizes 8d / 10d / 16d common and box (Table L4). Penetration default: fastener length − 1½" plate − ¾" subfloor on every floor above the base (length − 1½" at the base); the row prints "default … verify" until a value is entered. The same penetration sets the nail shear value where it is under 10D (Table 12N fn. 3), and a blank field is that default, not "specify". Disclosure: a nail driven through the bottom plate and the subfloor into the rim or plate below is not the two-member connection of Table 12N (side member 1½" on the main member); the tabulated value is used as-is. The SDS shear value assumes full thread penetration into the main member (C-C-2026 p. 377 note 2) and is used as-is when the default leaves ½" of thread in the plate. SDS ¼×4½ — NDS Table 12.2B as a No. 14 wood screw (ESR-2236 §4.1.3; C-C-2026 p. 377 note 4 uses the same 172 / 121 lb/in) over the 2¾" thread, capped by head pull-through through the wood bottom plate (C-C-2026 p. 377 note 5). Anchor bolts — the wood-side check is plate-washer bearing on the sill (Fc⊥, NDS Supplement Table 4A / 4B, no CD per Table 4.3.1, over the net washer area); the required tension per bolt Treq = t × s/12 is printed for the anchor rod / concrete check, which stays outside this calculation. Toe-nailed plates (Ctn 0.67) are not modelled — use the Manual source. Combined shear + uplift (NDS §12.4): the fastener at full-height sheathing carries vmax and t = vmax at once, so Z'α at α = 45° (Eq. 12.4-2 nails, Eq. 12.4-1 SDS) is checked against the resultant per fastener as its own row; it usually governs over the shear-only and uplift-only rows.

  • Sheathing: "Sheathing" grade wood structural panel, blocked construction assumed throughout. Gypsum wallboard is offered only as the opposite face of a wood structural panel wall (§4.3.2.3), is combined per §4.3.5.4.2, is limited to h/b of 2:1 (3.5:1 for wind design per Table 4.3.3 note 2) and is not counted in SDC E or F.
  • Chord lever arm is taken as Co·Σbi per §4.3.6.1.3 for a perforated wall and as the segment width bi per §4.3.6.1.2 for each segment of a segmented wall; the chords are assumed to sit at the extreme ends of the wall or segment. Compression is reported without dead-load relief, plus the accumulated point dead load carried on that end post.
  • Discontinuous wall lines: wall lines are correlated across levels by their id. A line that starts part way down the stack is accepted. A line that is missing at an intermediate level is a validation error unless transfer: true is set on that wall at the first level below the gap — the wall that receives the discontinuous load path. The transfer element itself (beam, truss or frame) is outside the scope of this calculation.
  • ASCE 7-16 Table 12.2-1 height limits are not evaluated. The selected system's limits are carried for reference, but this calculation has no building-height input, so the 35 ft limit on A.17 / B.24 in SDC D is not checked. Those systems are refused outright in SDC E and F, where Table 12.2-1 does not permit them.
