# Informational Edition Deltas

**Scope note on framing:** The calculators are being verified against **NDS 2018 / SDPWS 2021 / ASCE 7-16 / IBC 2021**. That means:
- Section 1 (NDS), Section 3 (ASCE 7), and Section 4 (IBC) below are genuinely *forward-looking* — they describe what would change only if the jurisdiction later adopts IBC 2024 / NDS 2024 / ASCE 7-22.
- Section 2 (SDPWS) is **not** forward-looking in the same sense. The calculator is supposed to already comply with SDPWS 2021, but its on-screen text/commentary still uses SDPWS 2015 wording and section numbers. That is a **present-day gap against the stated verification target**, not a future contingency. It is written here in the same delta-table format for consistency, but the engineer should treat it as a current finding, not a "someday" item.

Research is web-search/secondary-source based (AWC, STRUCTURE magazine, WoodWorks, ICC, Simpson SE Blog, and vendor/help-doc pages that quote the standards). Primary-text confirmation was not always obtainable (several official AWC/ICC PDFs returned non-extractable/garbled text or were paywalled/login-gated); those points are flagged **not confirmed** and should be checked against the actual purchased standard/commentary before the report is finalized.

## 1. NDS 2024 vs NDS 2018

| Provision | NDS 2018 | NDS 2024 | Effect on these calculators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shear design format, §3.4 (unnotched) | Stress-based: compare actual shear stress *f_v* to allowable shear stress *F_v'* | Reformatted to a force-based format: design shear force *V* compared to design shear resistance *V_r'* (algebraically equivalent, same numeric result) | Cosmetic/format-only for the calculator's math; if the calc reports intermediate stress values (f_v, F_v') on the output sheet, the labels would need updating to match 2024 nomenclature, but the pass/fail result is unchanged. **Not confirmed**: exact new variable names/equation numbers — could not get clean text of NDS 2024 §3.4. |
| Notched-member shear, §3.4.3 | Force-based (V vs. V_r) with geometry terms for notch location/depth — already force-based since the 2001 NDS | Same force-based approach, now consistent with the unnotched-member format (both use V/V_r') | No numeric change expected; only affects members with end notches, which is a secondary check some header/jamb configurations may trip. |
| Multi-ply connections, Chapter 12 / built-up members | Bolt/nail connections of multi-ply (built-up) columns/beams designed ply-to-ply without an explicit lateral load-distribution adjustment beyond existing yield-limit equations | AWC states "extensive changes" were made to "connections of multi-ply connections" in 2024; a diaphragm factor for nailed connections was **removed from Chapter 11** because its effect is already captured in SDPWS 2021 nominal unit shear values; explicit fastener head pull-through provisions added for nails/wood screws; explicit lateral design provisions added for smooth-shank and deformed-shank nails | Directly relevant to the multi-ply header/built-up column and stud-pack connections in the header/jamb/stud calculator. **Not confirmed** in detail — could not obtain readable NDS 2024 Chapter 12 text; flag for direct standard check before relying on any built-up-member connection factor carried over from 2018. |
| Chapter 16 (fire design cross-reference) | N/A (fire design was NDS Chapter 16, self-contained) | Chapter 16 revised to align with the new, separately published **2024 FDS** (Fire Design Specification) | Not applicable — these calculators do not appear to perform fire-resistance design. |
| Notching / taper-cut limitations | General notch geometry limits in §3.4.3, §4.4.3, etc. | New/clarified notching requirements limiting permissible notches and taper cuts across sawn lumber, glulam, and SCL | Could affect jamb/stud notch-for-plumbing checks if the calculator has any; **not confirmed** in numeric terms. |
| Column stability §3.7, beam-columns §3.9, bearing/C_b §3.10 | Existing C_P, interaction equation, and bearing-area factor provisions | No significant changes identified in any secondary source reviewed (AWC's own "Significant Changes in ANSI/AWC NDS-2024" course description was not accessible without an AWCLearn login, so this is based on absence of mention elsewhere, not a direct read of the standard) | **Not confirmed** — treat as "likely unchanged" rather than "confirmed unchanged." Worth a direct spot-check against the purchased 2024 NDS since these clauses drive the stud/jamb compression checks. |
| Sawn lumber adjustment factors: C_r (repetitive member, §4.3.9), C_F (size factor), C_fu (flat use) | C_r = 1.15 for 2"–4" thick, 2"+ wide members in assemblies of 3+ members ≤24" o.c.; C_F and C_fu per Table 4A/4D | No change to the 1.15 C_r value or its applicability criteria found in any source reviewed | **Not confirmed** as a change — no evidence found that these factors moved in 2024; absence of coverage in the "significant changes" search results is circumstantial, not a primary-source confirmation. |
| Table 2.3.2 load duration factors (C_D) | C_D = 1.6 (wind/seismic), 1.25 (snow), 1.15 (construction/L), 1.0 (normal/L), 0.9 (permanent/dead) | No change found in any secondary source | **Not confirmed** as unchanged — no source directly compared the table, but nothing flagged it as revised either. |
| 2024 NDS Supplement — reference design values, Table 4A/4F (visually graded dimension lumber) | DF-L No.2, SP No.2, SPF No.2 values per 2018 Supplement (SP values reflect the 2013 Southern Pine re-grading; DF-L/SPF unchanged since ~2012 regrading) | AWC's own Feb-2024 and Mar-2025 **Updates & Errata / Addendum** documents for the 2024 Supplement identify corrections to **Hem-Fir(N)** design values (Table 4A), Hem-Fir(N) specific gravity (Table 4C footnote 2), and HF/HF(N) values (Table 4G) — **Hem-Fir(N) only**. No DF-L, Southern Pine, or SPF No.2 changes were identified in the errata/addendum titles found. | If the calculator only offers DF-L, SP, and SPF species, the 2024 Supplement corrections likely do **not** affect its published Fb/Fv/Fc⊥/Fc/E/Emin values. **Not confirmed as complete** — could not open the actual errata PDF body (fetch returned unreadable binary); confirm no DF-L/SP/SPF entries were also revised, and confirm the calculator does not offer Hem-Fir. |

**What this means for the calculator:**
- No numeric red flags found for the species/grades the calculator most likely uses (DF-L No.2, SP No.2, SPF No.2) — the 2024 Supplement corrections identified so far target Hem-Fir(N) only.
- The most consequential item to verify directly against the purchased 2024 NDS text (not achievable via web search) is the **multi-ply connection provision change in Chapter 12**, since the header/jamb/stud calculator very likely nails or bolts multi-ply built-up members.
- The shear-design reformat (§3.4) is very likely cosmetic (same numeric answer, different variable names) but should be spot-checked against one hand calc.
- Column stability (§3.7), beam-column interaction (§3.9), bearing/C_b (§3.10), C_r, C_F, C_fu, and Table 2.3.2 duration factors show no evidence of substantive change in 2024 — treat as low-risk, but this is an absence-of-evidence conclusion, not a verified one.
- Recommend a direct check of the purchased 2024 NDS (or an AWCLearn login to the "Significant Changes in ANSI/AWC NDS-2024" course) rather than relying solely on this research pass for the connection-chapter items.

## 2. SDPWS 2021 vs SDPWS 2015 (current gap — calculator still uses 2015 wording)

| Provision | SDPWS 2015 | SDPWS 2021 | Effect on these calculators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit shear capacity tables (4.2A–D diaphragms, 4.3A/B/D shear walls) | Separate nominal unit shear values published for **wind** and **seismic** | Collapsed to a **single nominal unit shear value** used for both wind and seismic design | If the calculator's tables/lookup still carry separate wind/seismic columns per 2015, the underlying numbers and the ASD path (apply the correct single reduction factor below) are out of date relative to the 2021 target standard. |
| ASD reduction factors | 2.0 (wind), 2.8 (seismic) — applied to the (then wind- or seismic-specific) nominal value | Same 2.0 / 2.8 split, but now applied consistently to the single nominal value; **Table 4.3C gypsum wallboard values** in particular went from an effectively 2.0-based ASD value to a consistently-applied 2.8 seismic factor, cutting the ASD seismic capacity of gypsum board shear walls to roughly 70% of the prior (2015) value | Any header/jamb/wall calc that credits gypsum board as a seismic shear-resisting element per 2015 tables is now **non-conservative** relative to 2021 — this is the single highest-priority numeric item in this section. **Not confirmed** with exact before/after numbers; verify against SDPWS 2021 Table 4.3C directly. |
| LRFD resistance factors | Separate wind/seismic LRFD strengths | For seismic, ASD strengths are reported as roughly unchanged wall-to-wall, but LRFD strengths decreased approximately 11% for diaphragms/shear walls under the 2021 restructuring | Only relevant if the calculator offers an LRFD path; **not confirmed** — figure comes from a secondary summary, not the standard itself. |
| Perforated shear wall shear-capacity adjustment factor, C_o | Two allowed methods: Equations 4.3-5/4.3-6, **or** a lookup in Table 4.3.3.5 (engineer's choice) | Single, revised equation-based method (SDPWS 2021 Eq. 4.3-6 form) that better matches empirical test data across the full range of opening ratios; the table/equation choice was removed | If the calculator lets the user pick "table" vs. "equation" for C_o, or hard-codes the 2015 dual-path logic, that UI/logic choice no longer exists in 2021 and should be collapsed to the single method. |
| §4.3.2.3 perforated shear wall limitations list | Governing limitations (sheathing type consistency, full-height segment requirements, etc.) stated in fewer/older terms | List revised/expanded; clarifies that sheathing **around openings** must use the same nailing as full-height segments or be treated as opening area for C_o purposes | Directly affects how the calculator should treat partial-height sheathing around window/door openings adjacent to headers. **Not confirmed** verbatim — need the actual 2021 clause text. |
| Aspect ratio factor, §4.3.3.4 (SDPWS renumbering from earlier "4.3.4") | Two optional methods introduced in the **2015** edition itself (this was a 2012→2015 change, not 2015→2021): Equal-Deflection method, factor = 1.25 − 0.125(h/b_s); and the more conservative 2b_s/h method | Both methods were **carried forward unchanged into SDPWS 2021** per the source reviewed — i.e., 2015 and 2021 do not differ here | If the calculator's aspect-ratio logic matches the 2015-introduced methods, it should still be numerically correct under 2021; only the wording/section-number citation on the report may need updating (see renumbering row below). **This is the one item in this section where 2015 wording may not actually need a numeric fix — only a citation fix, if any.** |
| 10d common nail + hold-down-on-inside-face-of-end-post factor | No such explicit factor in 2015 | New footnote to Table 4.3A: **0.92 factor** applied to the nominal unit shear capacity for walls using 10d common nails where hold-downs are mounted on the inside face of the end post, reflecting reduced capacity observed in cyclic testing | This is a genuinely new 2021 requirement with no 2015 precedent. If the calculator's shear wall / hold-down module uses 10d nails with an inside-mounted hold-down and does not apply this 0.92 factor, it is **non-conservative**. High-priority item to verify. **Not confirmed** verbatim footnote text/number — flagged by a WoodWorks help note as "recently added...with the implementation of SDPWS 2021." |
| SDC D–F vertical strength distribution (new §4.1.8-type provision) | No equivalent explicit clause | New requirement prohibiting a lower story's lateral strength from being less than the story above's, unless the lower story's design strength exceeds the design load by the ratio V_r(i+1)/V_r(i) | Directly relevant to the **stacked perforated shear wall calculator**, which by definition deals with multi-story vertical load paths in SDC D–F. This is likely the single most consequential item in the whole SDPWS section for that calculator. **Not confirmed** exact section number — reviewed via a secondary summary, not the standard text itself. |
| Out-of-plane wind tables, Chapter 3 (Tables 3.2.1A, 3.2.2) | Combined/older nominal uniform load capacity tables | Expanded tables giving **separate OSB and plywood** nominal uniform load capacities, plus added coverage for W24/Structural I panels | Affects any out-of-plane wall-sheathing wind check the header/stud calculator performs; if it uses a single blended OSB/plywood value from 2015-era tables, that should be split per panel type under 2021. |
| Reference framing material, §4.1.2.1 | Implicit assumption of sawn lumber/glulam framing | Explicit statement that reference values assume sawn lumber or structural glued-laminated timber framing; SCL requires manufacturer data/ESR | Minor — relevant only if the calculator ever allows LVL/SCL wall studs as the shear wall framing member. |
| CLT diaphragms/shear walls, new §4.5/§4.6 | Not covered | New CLT-specific diaphragm and shear wall provisions (R = 3 or 4 for two defined CLT shear wall systems) | Not applicable — these calculators are wood-frame (dimension lumber/panel), not CLT. |
| Cantilevered diaphragm deflection, Table 4.2.3 | Prior deflection approach | Two new equations for diaphragm deflection at the end of a cantilevered diaphragm using apparent shear stiffness | Likely not applicable unless the calculators handle diaphragm deflection directly (they appear to be wall/header-focused). |

**What this means for the calculator:**
- This section is a **current compliance gap, not a future one** — the calculator is supposed to already reflect SDPWS 2021 but its text still cites 2015 wording/section numbers.
- Highest-priority numeric checks: (1) Table 4.3C gypsum board seismic ASD values (likely reduced under 2021), (2) the 0.92 nail/hold-down factor for 10d nails with an inside-mounted hold-down, (3) the SDC D–F vertical strength-distribution requirement for the stacked shear wall calculator, and (4) whether the calculator still offers the retired dual table/equation C_o method instead of the single 2021 equation.
- Lower-priority: aspect-ratio method appears unchanged 2015→2021 (only a possible section-number citation fix), and out-of-plane wind table granularity (OSB vs. plywood split).
- Several specific clause numbers/text strings above are flagged **not confirmed** because primary AWC/ICC PDF text could not be extracted (binary/garbled) or was login-gated; before editing the calculator, pull the actual SDPWS 2021 §4.1.8, §4.3.2.3, and Table 4.3A footnote 10 text directly.

## 3. ASCE 7-22 vs ASCE 7-16

| Provision | ASCE 7-16 | ASCE 7-22 | Effect on these calculators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chapter 2 load combinations — D, L, Lr, W, E factors | Standard ASD/LRFD combination sets; W factor already at 1.0W (post-7-10 Kd-baked-in convention); 0.6D+0.6W and 0.6D+0.7E as the governing uplift/overturning ASD combos | No evidence found of a change to the basic D/L/Lr/W/E numeric factors themselves; **0.6D+0.6W and 0.6D+0.7E appear to carry forward unchanged**. The load-combination *sections* were reorganized for clarity, and the digital-hazard-data requirement (below) changes how the inputs to W and E are derived, but not the combination arithmetic. | Low risk to the header/stud calculator's basic combo logic. **Not confirmed** by direct standard text — based on absence of any secondary source reporting a factor change, plus one low-confidence/self-contradictory search result that should be disregarded (it conflated the ASCE 7-05→7-10 wind-factor history with 7-16→7-22). |
| Chapter 2 — snow load combination factors | ASD: 1.0S; LRFD: 1.6S; importance factor I_s applied to ground/flat-roof snow | ASD: **0.7S**; LRFD: **1.0S**; importance factor **removed** — snow hazard is now "risk-targeted" (reliability-based ground snow values) similar in spirit to the seismic MCE_R approach, so the factor reduction offsets increased/redistributed ground snow values in the new maps | **Directly affects the header calculator's snow input.** If the calculator lets the user enter a ground/roof snow load and applies the old 1.0S (ASD) or 1.6S (LRFD) combination factor plus a user-entered I_s, that logic must change to 0.7S/1.0S with no I_s term once the jurisdiction adopts ASCE 7-22 snow maps and combos. |
| Chapter 7 — snow loads | Ground snow load from ASCE 7-16 maps (return-period based, county/local tables); importance factor I_s per risk category | New reliability/risk-targeted ground snow load values (first map update in ~30 years) built into the ASCE 7 Hazard Tool; drift/unbalanced snow load calculations revised using a physics-based "Winter Wind Parameter" model instead of the older empirically-derived approach; importance factor removed (see above) | Ground snow load values themselves will change (up or down depending on location) independent of the combination-factor change — the header calculator's snow input value, not just its combination factor, may need updating per jurisdiction once 7-22 maps are adopted. |
| Chapter 26–30 — wind, K_d relocation | K_d (wind directionality factor) embedded in the velocity pressure equation q_h = 0.00256·K_h·K_zt·K_d·K_e·V² | K_d **removed from the velocity pressure equation** (now q_h = 0.00256·K_h·K_zt·K_e·V²) and moved directly into the individual pressure/force equations, because a single building can have multiple K_d values depending on shape/component | For most single-shape buildings this is a net wash in the final pressure — reorganizational, not a magnitude change — but if the calculator's internal formula structure mirrors the old q_h-with-K_d equation literally (rather than treating K_d as a final multiplier), the intermediate variable would need restructuring even though the final answer should match. |
| Chapter 30 — Components & Cladding, low-rise wall Zones 4/5 | GC_p coefficients and zone geometry per 7-16 Figure 30.3-1 (or successor) | GC_p coefficients for C&C are, per STRUCTURE magazine, "typically either the same as ASCE 7-16 or slightly lower," so **resulting C&C wall design pressures are the same or slightly reduced** in most cases; roof zone count was also simplified (reduced from 5/4 zones to 3 zones for gable/hip roofs) — a roof-side change, not wall-side | For the header/stud calculator's C&C wall pressure input: expect **no increase, and commonly a small decrease** (source did not give a specific percentage) when a jurisdiction moves to 7-22. Wall Zone 4/5 geometry definitions (interior vs. corner zone width "a") were **not reported as changed** in the sources reviewed. **Not confirmed** — no numeric percentage was found in this pass; treat "slightly lower, or the same" as directional guidance only, not a value to hard-code. |
| Chapter 12 — seismic, Table 12.2-1 wood light-frame rows / height limits | Light-frame (wood) shear wall systems (bearing and non-bearing) with their existing R/Ω0/Cd values and height limits | No specific change to the wood light-frame rows' R/height-limit values was found in the sources reviewed. One related change: **redundancy factor exceptions for light-frame wood shear walls under §12.3.4.2 were reportedly narrowed/eliminated** in 7-22 per an Eng-Tips discussion thread (not a primary source) | **Not confirmed** — could not verify against the actual Table 12.2-1 text (paywalled ASCE Amplify content). If true, the redundancy-factor (ρ) exception change could affect the stacked shear wall calculator's seismic force level for wood-frame systems in SDC D–F; flag for direct standard check. |
| Chapter 12 — seismic, response spectrum / site class | Two-point spectrum defined by S_S and S_1; six site classes (A–F) | New **Multi-Period Response Spectrum (MPRS)** using 22 data points via the ASCE Hazard Tool (Method 1 for ELF base shear), with the traditional two-point spectrum retained as an alternate ("Method 2"); site classes expanded from 6 to **9**, with more emphasis on measured shear-wave velocity | Seismic base shear can no longer be derived from simple published S_S/S_1 map values by hand for the MPRS path — requires the online Hazard Tool. Overall seismic demand magnitude is reported as "similar to" 7-16 in aggregate, but can shift up or down at a given site. This is a workflow/input-source change for whatever module feeds the calculator's seismic design coefficients, more than a change to the wood-specific design equations. |
| Chapter 32 — tornado loads | Not a mandatory chapter | New mandatory tornado load provisions for Risk Category III/IV structures in tornado-prone regions | Not applicable to typical Risk Category I/II wood-frame single/multi-family header and shear wall design unless the calculator is ever used for a higher risk-category building in a tornado-prone region. |
| Simplified wind procedures | Simplified analytical wind procedures available for qualifying low-rise buildings | Reported as **removed** in 7-22, pushing designers to the full analytical procedure | If the calculator offers a "simplified" wind-pressure shortcut input path, that path would no longer have a 7-22 code basis. |

**What this means for the calculator:**
- Snow is the clearest, most quantifiable change: combination factors move to 0.7S (ASD)/1.0S (LRFD) with I_s removed, plus new ground snow values from updated maps — the header calculator's snow-load input and combination logic both need review under 7-22, not just the combination factor.
- Wind C&C wall pressures are expected to stay flat or drop slightly under 7-22 (directional finding only, no confirmed percentage) — low urgency, but the K_d relocation means the *formula structure*, not the *final number*, may need restructuring if the calculator's code mirrors the 7-16 q_h equation term-by-term.
- Seismic: base shear derivation source changes (MPRS via Hazard Tool, 9 site classes) affect upstream inputs more than downstream wood design; the reported/unconfirmed narrowing of the light-frame wood redundancy-factor exception is the one item worth a direct standard check given its relevance to the stacked shear wall calculator.
- Basic ASD/LRFD load-combination arithmetic (0.6D+0.6W, 0.6D+0.7E, 1.2D+1.0W+L+0.5(Lr or S), etc.) is not reported to have changed apart from the snow term — treat the rest of the combination set as low-risk.
- Tornado loads and the removed simplified wind method are edge cases unlikely to touch these two calculators directly.

## 4. IBC 2024 vs IBC 2021

| Provision | IBC 2021 | IBC 2024 | Effect on these calculators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Referenced wood design standard | ANSI/AWC **NDS-2018** | ANSI/AWC **NDS-2024** | Confirms the premise of Section 1 above — a jurisdiction moving to IBC 2024 pulls in NDS 2024 automatically. |
| Referenced lateral wood standard | AWC **SDPWS-2021** (via §2305.1) | AWC **SDPWS-2021** continues to be the referenced edition (no newer SDPWS edition was found to exist yet as of this research) | No change here — reinforces that Section 2's SDPWS 2015→2021 gap is a *current*, not future, issue: SDPWS 2021 is already the target under both IBC 2021 and IBC 2024. |
| Referenced hazard standard | ASCE 7-16 | ASCE 7-22 | Pulls in all of Section 3's changes automatically once a jurisdiction adopts IBC 2024. |
| §2305.1 terminology | "Wood-frame shear walls and diaphragms" | Changed to **"wood shear walls" and "wood diaphragms"** (dropping "-frame") specifically to also cover CLT shear walls/diaphragms now included in SDPWS | Terminology-only for a dimension-lumber-only calculator; no numeric effect. |
| §2305/2306 permanent-load-duration shear wall provision | Design unit shear capacities for wood shear walls/diaphragms resisting permanent-duration lateral loads (no wind/seismic combination) taken from SDPWS nominal values | Same substantive provision carried forward: nominal unit shear capacities multiplied by **0.2** for ASD (§2306) and **0.3** for LRFD (§2307) when the lateral load is permanent-duration only | Edge case (permanent lateral load without wind/seismic) — likely not triggered by typical header/shear-wall design scenarios in these calculators, but worth confirming the calculator doesn't need this path. **Not confirmed** whether this is new language in 2024 or carried over verbatim from 2021 — could not get a clean side-by-side of §2305/2306 text. |
| Table 1604.3 deflection limits — footnote on creep / 0.5D provision | For dry-installed, dry-service sawn lumber/glulam/I-joist/SCL members designed per NDS, creep component of long-term deflection may be estimated as the immediate dead-load deflection from **0.5D** (not combined with NDS long-term-loading provisions); for other moisture conditions, or for CLT/wood structural panels, use **D** (not 0.5D) | Language located in the 2024 IBC text online matches the same substantive 0.5D/D creep-estimation approach; **no change identified** between the 2021 and 2024 wording in the sources checked | Low risk — if the calculator's deflection-limit footnote text already reflects the 0.5D creep provision correctly for 2021, it should not need updating for 2024. **Not confirmed by direct side-by-side** — the IBC 2021 page returned an access error (403) during this research pass, so the comparison relies on the 2024 text alone plus the absence of any "significant changes" article flagging a 1604.3 wood-footnote revision. |
| §2308 / §2305–2306 wood provisions touching shear walls or headers | Prescriptive wood-frame provisions (braced wall lines, conventional light-frame construction) | No specific header- or shear-wall-relevant prescriptive change was identified beyond the §2305.1 terminology update above; IBC 2024 continues to permit the SDPWS engineered path as an alternative to prescriptive §2308 methods | **Not confirmed** as exhaustive — a full line-by-line 2308 comparison was not obtained; if the calculator ever cross-references prescriptive §2308 span/bracing tables (as opposed to pure engineered NDS/SDPWS design), that comparison should be done directly against the ICC significant-changes series (Part 7, wood) which could not be extracted cleanly in this pass. |

**What this means for the calculator:**
- IBC 2024's main structural relevance here is simply that it is the *delivery mechanism* for NDS 2024 and ASCE 7-22 — Sections 1 and 3 are the substantive content; Section 4 itself adds little new beyond terminology.
- SDPWS reference stays at 2021 in both IBC 2021 and IBC 2024 — this reinforces that Section 2's findings are a current gap against an already-adopted standard, not a future one.
- Table 1604.3's wood creep/0.5D footnote appears unchanged, but this was not confirmed via a clean side-by-side (2021 IBC page blocked the fetch); low priority to re-verify given no third-party source flagged a change.
- The permanent-load-duration shear wall provision (0.2 ASD / 0.3 LRFD multiplier) is unlikely to be exercised by typical header/jamb/stud or wind/seismic shear wall runs but should be confirmed out-of-scope for the calculators rather than assumed.
- No prescriptive §2308 change directly touching headers or shear walls was found, but this area was not exhaustively reviewed — flag for a follow-up pass against the ICC "2024 IBC Significant Structural Changes — Wood" article (STRUCTURE magazine / ICC Structural Changes Part 7) once a text-extractable copy is available.

## Overall priority summary for the engineer

1. **SDPWS 2015→2021 wording in the calculator is a current defect, not a future one** — the target standard is already 2021. Highest-priority checks: Table 4.3C gypsum board seismic values, the 0.92 factor for 10d nails with inside-mounted hold-downs, the retired dual C_o method, and (for the stacked wall calculator specifically) the new SDC D–F vertical strength-distribution requirement.
2. **If/when the jurisdiction moves to NDS 2024**, the multi-ply connection provisions (Chapter 12) are the item most likely to need calculator logic changes; the shear-design reformat (§3.4) is very likely cosmetic only; species/grade design values for DF-L, SP, and SPF No.2 do not appear to be affected by the 2024 Supplement errata (Hem-Fir(N) only, as found).
3. **If/when the jurisdiction moves to ASCE 7-22**, snow load combination factors and ground snow values are the clearest required calculator change; wind C&C wall pressures should stay flat or drop slightly (unconfirmed magnitude); seismic base-shear derivation moves to the Hazard Tool/MPRS but wood-specific design equations are not reported to change.
4. **IBC 2024** itself mainly just delivers NDS 2024/ASCE 7-22; no wood-specific IBC provision was found to independently affect the calculators beyond that pass-through, though the §2308 prescriptive comparison and the exact §2305/2306 permanent-load-duration text were not exhaustively verified.
5. Numerous individual clause numbers and a few percentages above are marked **not confirmed** because primary AWC/ICC PDFs could not be extracted as readable text in this research pass (corrupted/garbled extraction) or were login/paywall-gated (AWCLearn, ASCE Amplify).
